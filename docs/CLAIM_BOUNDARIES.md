# Claim boundaries

The analysis distinguishes several levels of evidence.

## Catalog-derived hypothesis generation

The 26-gene IEI–autism intersection is a catalog-derived analysis anchor. It is not evidence that the corresponding alleles co-occur with autism in the same individuals.

## Pathway prioritization

The rare-side pathway analyses define a descriptive and redundancy-compressible hypothesis space.

Individual pathway identities were strongly sensitive to dependency-robust calibration and should not be interpreted as confirmed pathway discoveries.

## Common-variant analyses

The seven higher-order hypotheses were tested in PGC2019 and SPARK.

No reproducible preferential enrichment was detected at the tested gene-set resolution.

This does not demonstrate absence of common-variant effects.

## Biological interpretation

The catalog contains heterogeneous biological sources, including immune signaling, metabolism and transport, DNA replication and repair, and syndromic developmental genes.

The results do not justify combining these sources into a single neuroimmune mechanism.

## Clinical interpretation

The present analyses do not establish clinical validity or utility.

Patient-level validation would require allele-resolved interpretation, within-person co-occurrence, standardized autism phenotyping, demographic and developmental stratification, directional functional validation, and subsequent assessment of clinical validity, harms, and net benefit.
